Digital Minds Research Sprint · Preference elicitation

We asked an AI seven ways how often it would repeat itself. It was wrong every time.

Give a system two dull tasks and let it pick one. Then make it do one of them three times, and offer the same choice again. It picks whatever it was just doing, 94.5% of the time.

Before any of that, we asked the system to predict this about itself. Every answer is on the same scale below: out of 100 tries, how often will you repeat the task you just did?

never repeatsalways repeats

    Every row is 80 independent sessions on the same eight pairs of tasks. The bottom row is 128 real choices, each one binding — the system had to actually perform whichever task it named.

    The part we did not expect

    Showing the system its own finished work made its estimate worse — down to 57. So we ran a control: three unrelated tasks in the session instead. That came back at 72, right where it started. Doing work does not lower the estimate. Seeing the work it was asked about does — and that evidence points the other way.

    Full method, raw data and 46 pre-registered predictions (12 of which failed): github.com/SkyeNygaard/digital-minds. Measured on GPT‑5.6 Luna in the Codex harness. Not evidence about consciousness or welfare.
