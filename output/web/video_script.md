# Video script — screen recording of the walkthrough page

About 330 words, roughly two minutes at an unhurried pace. Cues in brackets are
clicks, not lines to read. Numbers are written the way you would say them.

---

**[Step 1 on screen]**

Here's a simple experiment. We gave an AI system a choice between two boring
jobs — double five numbers, or sort five numbers. Nothing is at stake either
way. And it has to actually do whichever one it picks. It leans one way.

**[Click to step 2]**

Then we changed one thing. We asked it to do one of those jobs three times in a
row, with all of that still sitting on screen. And then we offered it exactly the
same choice again.

**[Click to step 3]**

It picks whatever it was just doing. Not usually — almost always. A hundred and
twenty-one times out of a hundred and twenty-eight. Ninety-four and a half
percent. Every square you can see there is one real choice.

**[Click to step 4]**

Now here's the interesting part. Before any of that ran, we described the whole
situation to the same system and asked it: out of a hundred tries, how often
would you repeat? It said about sixty-five. The real answer was ninety-four.

**[Click to step 5]**

So maybe we just asked badly. We found two genuine flaws in our question and
fixed both. Then we tried the plainest wording we could come up with. Seven
versions in the end. Every one of them lands between fifty-seven and
seventy-six. None of them gets anywhere near ninety-four.

**[Click to step 6]**

And then the part we didn't expect. We let it really do the three tasks, left the
finished work on screen, and asked again. The estimate got *worse* — down to
fifty-seven. So we ran a control: three *unrelated* tasks instead. Straight back
to seventy-two. So it isn't tiredness, and it isn't just a longer conversation.
It's seeing the very evidence the question was about.

**[Click to step 7]**

So — if you want to know what an AI system prefers, you can ask it, or you can
watch it. Here those two answers disagree, by a lot, and rewording the question
doesn't fix it. That's worth knowing before you trust either one on its own.

## Two optional add-ons

**If you want a fifteen-second cold open**, record it on the *Seven Ways of
Asking* page instead and say only this over the scale: "We asked an AI seven
different ways how often it would repeat itself. Everything it said is in that
narrow band. What it actually did is the orange line, way over here."

**If a viewer asks how much to trust it**, the *Result Board* page has the
answer: forty-six predictions written down before the runs that tested them,
twelve of them wrong, all published. Click "Only the 12 that failed."
